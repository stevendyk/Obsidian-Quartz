## Chapter Summaries

#### Chapter 1 - A Crisis of Identity

_Entire Sanctification_ or _Christian perfection_ is the grand depositum that God
has given to us (Methodists). - John Wesley

John Wesley's definition of Entire Sanctification:

1. that love of God and our neighbor, which implies deliverance from all sin.
2. That this is received merely by faith.
3. That it is given instantaneously, in one moment.
4. That we are to expect it, not at death, but every moment; that now is the accepted time, now is the day of this salvation.

#### Chapter 2 - Methodism's Purpose _Video - Wisdom's Purpose_

##### Steps - Repentance, Faith, Holiness (Sanctification)

Entire sanctification as process and event.
The call to discipleship is to lay down your life - the entirety of our lives.
Wesley experienced revival many times.

Practice: small groups - class and bands.
We believe that real transformation is possible, by God's grace, and with the support of each other.

#### Chapter 3 - Glimpsing Real Methodism

Key Concepts

- Methodism was organized to spread holiness.
- The band meeting may be the most important context where Methodists gather together seeking holiness.
- The goal was for Christians to realize that God wants to bring them entirely into alignment with the Spirit's will for their lives.
- Entire sanctification is not a sign that one is an elite or super-spiritual Christian. Rather, it comes as a response to awareness of great need for God to do in us what we cannot do in ourselves.

#### Chapter 4 - What Happened?

History of Methodist splits over conservative adherence to Wesleyan roots of entire sanctification, and movement toward liberal (German) theology.
Confidence that entire sanctification is possible, and has been personally experienced.
Reiteration that sanctification is entirely a gift from God.
