#### Why are there Methodists?

John Wesley's belief in why God had raised up a people called Methodists was that they were called to save souls, to reform the larger church, and to spread scriptural holiness across the land. — location: [356](kindle://book?action=open\&asin=B004U5GB6S\&location=356) ^ref-60580

---

Wesley always spoke of #holiness of heart and life because he believed that anyone who was truly being saved by grace would exhibit it both in the inward affections of the heart and the outward pattern of life. Holiness is the life defined by love of God and neighbor. — location: [366](kindle://book?action=open\&asin=B004U5GB6S\&location=366) ^ref-53623

---

They understood that their salvation was not something that waited on the other side of death but was instead a thing to be received in the present. So they agreed with Wesley himself, as he said, "By salvation I mean, not barely . . . deliverance from hell, or going to heaven, but a present deliverance from sin, a restoration of the soul to its primitive health, its original purity; a recovery of the divine nature; the renewal of our souls after the image of God in righteousness and true holiness, in justice, mercy, and truth." Perhaps most importantly, they understood that the offer of such a gift called for a response of their entire lives. Knowing that their sanctification was a process that happened over time as they were progressively renewed by grace, they practiced their faith. — location: [375](kindle://book?action=open\&asin=B004U5GB6S\&location=375) ^ref-52398

#### The means of grace

are practices that draw us closer to Jesus, and as practices they are repeated time and time again. They encompass the whole of our discipleship. — location: [398](kindle://book?action=open\&asin=B004U5GB6S\&location=398) ^ref-34530

---

Wesley tended to speak of the means of grace in the two basic categories of works of piety and works of mercy. By works of piety, he meant those practices that direct our attention to God. They show us what it means to fulfill the first great commandment: to "love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your mind, and with all your strength" (Mark 12:30). — location: [407](kindle://book?action=open\&asin=B004U5GB6S\&location=407) ^ref-49124

---

Works of mercy are the other category of the means of grace. Wesley called these "prudential" means of grace because, while every example of them might not be found in Scripture, we discover them to be true means of grace by prudence—or that practical wisdom gained from the experience of living out our discipleship. — location: [419](kindle://book?action=open\&asin=B004U5GB6S\&location=419) ^ref-34251

---

They show us what it means to fulfill the second great commandment: to "love your neighbor as yourself " (Mark 12:31). By them we reach out to the poor and needy, we show hospitality to the stranger, and we comfort the sick and afflicted. Through committing ourselves to works of mercy, we both fulfill Jesus' command to love our neighbor and find that the very act of doing such works increases God's love within us! — location: [422](kindle://book?action=open\&asin=B004U5GB6S\&location=422) ^ref-47762

---

Two important conclusions can be drawn from this description. The first is that the means of grace must be fully utilized in a committed, disciplined way. The means of grace are the pattern of the Christian life. They are not one-shot acts that can be done once and then left behind. They are the substance of discipleship itself, and so they must be engaged in regularly—even daily! — location: [437](kindle://book?action=open\&asin=B004U5GB6S\&location=437) ^ref-59958

---

The second conclusion is apparent when we look at how the means of grace happen: namely, they must be located (and so we must be located) in the church. — location: [440](kindle://book?action=open\&asin=B004U5GB6S\&location=440) ^ref-17528
